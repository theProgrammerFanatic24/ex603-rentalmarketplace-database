
Assignment 5: Rental Marketplace Findings and Recommendations

To: Rental Marketplace Leadership
From: Trust and Data Team
Subject: Improving Property Viewing Activity and Addressing Renter No-Shows


EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Our analysis identified two different groups of properties that require management attention: properties experiencing viewing failures and properties with no recorded viewing activity.

These groups should not be treated the same way. Properties experiencing renter no-shows have attracted scheduled viewings but may face problems with attendance. Properties with no viewings have not recorded any viewing activity and may require attention to their listings or visibility.

The database confirms that Property_14 and Property_15 have no recorded viewings. The earlier performance analysis identified properties with elevated failure rates, which should be investigated separately.


FINDING 1: PROPERTIES EXPERIENCING VIEWING FAILURES

Properties with elevated renter no-show rates may create scheduling inefficiencies and missed opportunities for property managers.

However, a no-show does not automatically mean that the property or its manager caused the problem. Renter availability, scheduling conflicts, transportation difficulties, and communication issues may contribute.

Recommendation:

Leadership should review the viewing histories of properties identified as having elevated no-show rates.

The marketplace could introduce or improve appointment reminders, request viewing confirmations, and examine whether certain appointment times are associated with more missed visits.

After implementing changes, leadership should compare no-show rates over time to determine whether the interventions improve attendance.


FINDING 2: PROPERTIES WITHOUT VIEWING ACTIVITY

The LEFT JOIN analysis returned all 15 properties, while the INNER JOIN returned only 13.

Property_14 (property_id 14) and Property_15 (property_id 15) were excluded from the INNER JOIN because neither property had a matching viewing record.

Both NOT EXISTS and EXCEPT independently identified the same two properties.

These results confirm an absence of recorded viewings, but they do not establish the reason.

Recommendation:

Leadership should review Property_14 and Property_15 to confirm that their listings are published, available, accurate, and visible to renters.

The team should also examine rental prices, locations, listing descriptions, and listing dates.

If these properties are available but receiving little attention, improving their listing information or promotional visibility may help attract renters.


WHY THE GROUPS REQUIRE DIFFERENT ACTIONS

Properties with high no-show rates have recorded viewing activity, so the priority is improving attendance and reducing failed appointments.

Properties with no viewing activity require a different investigation. The priority is determining whether renters can discover and schedule viewings for those properties.

Applying the same intervention to both groups would risk addressing the wrong problem.


DATA LIMITATIONS

The analysis establishes which properties have recorded viewings and which do not. It also allows the marketplace to investigate viewing outcomes.

However, the available results do not independently establish why renters miss appointments or why particular properties have no recorded viewings.

Leadership should verify the underlying causes before making decisions about individual properties, managers, or compensation.


FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Leadership should establish two separate improvement efforts.

First, investigate properties with elevated renter no-show rates and test scheduling or communication improvements.

Second, review Property_14 and Property_15 for listing visibility, availability, and potential barriers to booking.

Both efforts should be monitored using accurate reports that include properties with and without viewing records.

This approach would give leadership a more complete understanding of marketplace activity and support decisions based on verified evidence rather than assumptions.
